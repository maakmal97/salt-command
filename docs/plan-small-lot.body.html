A small lot no longer sets a book's Floor

Today a book's cost is its latest landed purchase, whatever its size. So candy's 1-unit lot (p057, MYR 69 and MYR 25 freight, MYR 94 a unit) sets candy's Floor and lifts every candy price to MYR 95 a unit, over its MYR 60 board. From this version, a lot with fewer units than its book's minimum is blended with the lots before it: cost is what the newest units, up to that minimum, cost. One function changes, so every reader follows.

How the engine does it one function, so no reader can miss it

  • engine/rules.mjs gains COST_MIN = {salt: 12.5, oil: 10, candy: 10, rice: 100, gas: 1}, carried in PRICING_RULES. A book it does not name has a minimum of nought, which is today's rule. It is a rule, not ledger data, so it is added to NOT_LEDGER in tools/ledger.mjs (the gate refuses a data-shaped name in the master that is not classified), and to the names test/engine-rules.mjs pins for PRICING_RULES. It stays out of the PRICING snapshot's inputs, so no fixture changes for it.
  • engine/position.mjs latestCogs(purchases, onOrBefore) keeps its signature and reads the minimum itself, by the lots' book (an absent product means salt, docs/PRODUCTS.md section 6). The import is written exactly import PRICING_RULES from "./rules.mjs";, the one form tools/engine.mjs --sync drops. Any other name (RULES, say) would bind the master's other rules table, and the Desk would keep today's rule without any error. rules.mjs imports nothing, so there is no cycle, and in the master the rules block already stands before the position block.
  • The lots it may read are exactly today's: live (not pending, defaulted or cancelled), with something received, landed on or before the day. On one day, the later in the list counts as the newer, and a partly landed lot counts over its received units.
  • When the newest lot reaches the minimum, it answers the same object as today, byte for byte.
  • Otherwise it takes lots newest first, each at its own rate, the last only for the units still needed. If all the landed lots together fall short of the minimum, they are all taken whole.
  • What it answers: p, rid and date stay the newest lot's, so the non-revenue anchor and the order fault's date do not move. quote and freight are the shares counted, units the units counted, and rate is their sum over the units, to four places, as now. A new field, lots ([{rid, date, units, rate, draft}]), names each lot, the units taken from it and its own rate. It is present only when a blend is in force, both here and in its two copiers (engine/snapshot.mjs and engine/pricing.mjs), so every fixture that carries latest stays equal.
Why inside the function, not from each callerEight call sites read latestCogs. Passing the minimum in from each caller would let one of them forget it, and two figures for one cost is the drift that rule 1 forbids.

How it is proved

  • A new section, "a small lot does not set the Floor" (test/floor-small-lot.mjs). Each check is proved red by its own mutation before its green counts.
  • The suite's frozen book (test/fixtures/book-frozen.json) ends at p039 (13 Oct 2026), before candy's small lot, so nothing blends on it. On that book, every reading must stay identical to today's: that is the check that nothing else moved.
  • Candy's case is carried in the section itself: lots p027, p031, p057 and p062 written out as literals. They must answer MYR 47.20 over 10 units, naming 1 unit of p057 and 9 of p031, and MYR 30 once p062 lands.
  • Three mutations go red: counting the last lot whole (MYR 45.25), taking the oldest lot first, and ignoring the minimum (MYR 94).
  • The page against the module: on a temporary SALT_BOOK holding those lots, and a master rendered from it with syncText (as test/frozen.mjs does) passed as SALT_MASTER, the Desk's master in jsdom, the module and the drafter all give candy MYR 47.20. So a Desk that kept the old rule (an import bound to the wrong name) is caught.
  • Edge cases: lots that together fall short of the minimum are taken whole; a lot after the day, or a pending, defaulted or cancelled lot, is never counted in a blend; a partly landed lot counts over its received units; the same-day order holds; and a book COST_MIN does not name keeps today's rule.
  • On the snapshot of that temporary book: candy's Floor is MYR 50 at 1 unit and its board MYR 60. Pricing parity (the book's inputs against the page) stays equal on both books.
  • The drafter's note names each lot, and a drafted lot second in the blend raises the flag.
  • The grep check: no other copy of the cost arithmetic.
  • Then the full suite, CI's run on the push, and the Counter drawn with counter-render to read candy's prices.

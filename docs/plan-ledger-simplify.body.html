2. Every sale has its order Opus

The rule. Every live customer sale is named by at least one live site order, or by a shared group whose sums equal the row (the s218 and s219 shape). A row no order names is listed on the Ledger check, apart from a named list: s182, s235 and s236 (owing) and s234 (their trip), each leaving the list when it settles.

Not a fault, its own list. The Ledger check shows these as orderless, outside its faults. The import still needs a clean Ledger check, so a fault would have blocked the very import that cures it (Fable's first high finding).

First, a table. Every road that mints a sale (Accept, the import, Salt Admin's sale, redeem, partner and edit, a trip's split and join, the offset, the reward and loyalty units, a hand fold) is listed with whether it makes an order, is excluded, or counts as orderless. Nothing is refused until that table is filed.

The recovery road. A sale with no order is refused ("Order for them"), unless a new one-row D1 switch sale_recovery is on. Such a sale carries a noOrder flag on its card and stays on the list until it is imported.

The version. Six commits, the full suite. One check per exclusion reason; the s395 shape counts as named; a shared group counts as named; an orderless row leaves the Ledger check clean and can still be imported; the refusal and the switch.

Way back. Turn the switch on, or revert.

3. Follow every order by rid Fable

Why. Today an order finds its row by party|date|total. When two sales share that key, or a Correction moves it, the order can lose its row: that was the v1551 incident. The imported orders already follow by rid; this makes it every order's road.

Version A, additive.

  • One reader, rowFor, rid first and the key as a counted fallback. Every place that now reads a row by key goes through it: the follow, the return leg, the Ledger check, the reconcile, the money on its way, Accept's preview, the import and the claim allocation.
  • The rid is stamped from the row the follow resolves, never from the Accept draft, and never onto a cancelled row. It is stamped only while the Ledger check is clean, and only once the draft is booked.
  • On the Counter the rid is set once, moved only by a mark naming both rids, and never shown to a customer.
  • A backfill tool, dry first, for older orders.
  • Before building: every sale on the Book has a rid, and a trip's split, join and compaction keep rids.
  • Cut from the tip after item 2 lands, since both edit the Ledger check.

Version B, a week later. After seven days in which no order that should carry a rid fell back to its key (accepted, its row on the book, not a test account), the key roads go: strayRow, followFree, sharedOk and the shared-key cases. Rule 5's wording follows.

Way back. Version A adds a road and removes none. rowRoom and payFault are untouched.

4. Tell only the orders a row names Opus

Now. After every tap, the outbox runs a whole forced pass over every order, and the regular minute pass then runs whole again, because every tap moves the Book's revision. That is two whole passes a tap.

Before building. Both switches, book_outbox and book_tap, read on.

The change. The forced pass is limited to the orders the booked rows name: the entry's own order, a claim's orders, those whose rid or key matches the row, and every order of the row's customer but the cancelled and declined. That last set covers credit, an account claim, and a finished order an amendment reopens. Shared groups are built over all orders before the limit, and a key collision is still weighed against everyone. An empty list runs the whole pass, never nothing.

The net stays. The regular pass is unchanged and still runs on every revision move. The forced pass never moves the told mark, so undo and reward rows keep their net.

The version. Three commits, the full suite, after item 3A. Checks: a tap tells its own order and no other; an empty list runs whole; a collision outside the list still blocks a follow; a second order of the customer hears its credit; a missed order is told by the regular pass.

Way back. Revert, or switch book_outbox off.

What stays: applyInto, noteFallback, booksync, the export, /book/load?import=1, point-in-time recovery.

Way back. From 5a on, recovery is the sealed export, an import load, or point-in-time recovery. The rehearsed flip back ends at 5a, so any waiting month comes before it.

What stays, and what was rejected

The Book deriving from the Counter's order book would put cost and margin behind the customers' door, copy the engine into stmt/ where two engines drift (rule 1), and reverse your answer of 25 Oct 2026 that orders and the book stay two objects. Half the entry kinds have no order anyway.

Rejected: merging the approval roads. They already share one booking (bookTap).

One price band for salt

The Floor is what salt truly costs you, the Ceiling is the most the market has shown it will bear, and the Set Price is the figure between them that earns the most. One scale runs from -100% (the Floor, a partner's price) through 0% (the Set Price) to +100% (the Ceiling). Plan only: nothing is built or changed.

The answer

How each price is determined

  1. Floor. The latest purchase's cost a unit, plus the share of salt that left unpaid, times the size, up to the next RM 5. Today RM 50 plus 21.3%, so RM 60.65 a unit. Live since v1496; unchanged here.
  2. Ceiling. Your curve through three points you named: RM 90 at 0.5 unit, RM 180 at 1 unit and RM 1,250 at 12.5 units. Held as multiples of cost (3.6 times up to 1 unit, sliding to 2 times at 12.5), so it follows cost.
  3. Set Price. A base read from what 250 paid sales show earns the most at each size, then moved a few points up or down each day by stock cover (supply) and the pace of orders (demand).
  4. Their %. A customer's place on the scale. Each 10 points is the same percentage step in price, not the same ringgit step, so the scale is even from end to end.

Small lots earn

Margin a unit over the row's own cost: RM 72 at 0.5 unit, RM 52 at 1 to 2 units, RM 41 at 2.2 to 3, RM 34 at 4 to 5, RM 27 near 6.25 and RM 14 at 10 units and over. Lots of 2 units or fewer are 28% of the units and 44% of the margin.

Bulk is under cost

The usual 12.5-unit sale is RM 750. The Floor with unpaid salt in it is RM 760. Sold at the usual price, a bulk lot now loses about RM 10.

Set Price runs low

From 1.5 to 5 units customers usually pay more than today's Set Price (RM 170 against RM 140 at 1.5; RM 450 against RM 425 at 5). At 12.5 they usually pay less than it.

Price barely moves demand

Across 18 full weeks, a week priced 10% higher sold about 3% fewer units (estimate, weak: correlation -0.09). After paying over 8% above the usual price, 76% of buyers came back within 30 days, against 78% at the usual price and 94% at a low one.

But high prices cost orders

Orders priced over 8% above usual were cancelled 12% of the time against 6.5% at the usual price, and the next order after a high price was smaller: 1.8 units against 2.7.

You already price scarcity

With 3 days of stock or fewer (42 of 137 days) sales went at 7% over the usual price; with over 14 days of stock, 4% under. Sales a day did not fall when stock was short (4.7 units against 5.1). Estimate: the daily shelf is rebuilt from purchases, handovers and counts.

The 0.5 Ceiling binds

Four 0.5-unit sales sit on exactly RM 75, today's Ceiling. The rule stopped the price, so the book cannot say what those buyers would have paid.

One buyer's own range

The same buyer, at the same size, has paid 13% apart between their lowest and highest price in the usual case, and 38% apart at the wide end (31 cases of three or more sales).

Floor

True cost, the price you never sell under

Live since v1496. No change proposed.
  1. Rule. Cost a unit (the latest purchase's quote plus its freight, over its units) times 1 plus the unpaid share, times the size, the order total up to the next RM 5.
  2. The unpaid share is count shortfall, gifts and rewards, and written-off salt, over the units paid for. Today 114.34 + 15.39 + 2.50 over 620.92 units: 21.3%.
  3. Who pays it. A partner, at every size. On the new scale that is simply -100%.
  4. One refinement to settle. The share ticks with every handover. Re-measuring it only at a stock count or a new lot would keep the Floor still between them (Ask F).

Set Price

The price that earns the most, moved by the market

Step 1. The base, from the evidence

For each size, every price from the Floor up is scored by profit a unit times the share of that size's buying done by customers who have paid that price or more. The best score is the profit-best price. It is cautious by design: a customer who paid RM 100 may well have paid RM 110, so the true best is at or above it.

The scale

From -100% to +100%

Replaces today's -30% to +30%.

A customer's price is found by their place between three posts: the Floor, today's Set Price and the Ceiling. Above 0%, the price is the Set Price times the ratio of Ceiling to Set Price, raised to their %. Below 0%, the same with the Floor. In plain terms: every 10 points up is the same percentage rise, and every 10 points down the same percentage cut, at that size.

Keeping 0% honest

How the Set Price stays at the profit-best price

  1. A standing reading on Pricing. For each size, over the last 90 days: the usual price paid, the profit-best price, and the Set Price beside them, with the count of sales behind each.
  2. A re-anchor is your tap. When the reading and the base differ by RM 5 or more at a size with 10 or more sales, the Desk suggests the new point. Nothing re-anchors by itself.
  3. Losses are counted, not guessed. Cancellations and smaller next orders after a high price are read into the same reading, so a price that earns more a sale but loses the next one shows.
  4. The book records every quote from here on. Today it keeps only what sold. Recording a quote that did not turn into an order is the one thing that would turn this from a cautious estimate into a measured demand curve.
Limits

What the data cannot say

  1. 46 buyers, and five of them are 40% of the margin. A handful of people set these curves. Sixteen bought once.
  2. Only prices that sold are in the book. The lost-demand log is empty, so the profit-best figures are floors on the true answer.
  3. The Ceiling capped small lots, so willingness to pay above RM 75 at 0.5 unit is unseen.
  4. Demand's response to price is weak and noisy: 18 weeks, inside a range of about 15% either side of usual. Outside that range it is unknown.
  5. Bulk has 10 sales. Its base stays where the Floor forces it.
  6. The market-move points are starting values. The supply ones rest on your own past pricing; the demand ones on nothing measured yet.

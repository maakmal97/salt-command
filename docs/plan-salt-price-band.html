One price band for salt

The Floor is what salt truly costs you, the Ceiling follows your supplier's price, and the Median Price (today's Set Price, renamed) is the figure between them that earns the most. One scale runs from -100% (the Floor) through 0% (the Median) to +100% (the Ceiling). Around the band sits an engine that reads stock, pace and each customer's habit. Version 1, the band, is live as v1500 (11 Nov 2026) together with answer H; versions 2 and 3 are not built.

The answer

How each price is determined

  1. Floor. The latest purchase's cost a unit, plus the share of salt that left unpaid, times the size, up to the next RM 5. Today RM 50 plus 21.3%, so RM 60.65 a unit. Live since v1496; unchanged here.
  2. Ceiling. Struck on your supplier's price for a 12.5-unit lot (RM 56 a unit): 3.57 times it at 0.5 unit, sliding to 1.79 times at 12.5, then gently to 1.50 times at 100. Today RM 200 a unit at 0.5 and RM 100 at 12.5, your two figures.
  3. Median Price. The price a stranger is quoted, 0% on the scale. It starts at today's prices, is re-anchored from what sales show earns the most, and moves a few points with stock and pace.
  4. Their %. A customer's place on the scale. Each 10 points is the same percentage step in price at that size.
  5. The engine. Price is the smaller lever. 20 regular buyers are 82% of the margin and their habits are predictable, so the engine's main work is keeping them on time and telling you when new buyers dry up.

The Median runs ahead of the evidence

Since the 20% rise of 10 Nov (v1497) the Median sits above the usual price paid at every size but 1.5 units: RM 120 against RM 100 at 1 unit, RM 625 against RM 500 at 6.25. From 2.5 units up it is above anything yet paid. It is untested; the first four weeks are the test.

Small lots earn

Margin a unit over the row's own cost: RM 72 at 0.5 unit, RM 52 at 1 to 2 units, RM 41 at 2.2 to 3, RM 34 at 4 to 5, RM 27 near 6.25 and RM 14 at 10 units and over. Lots of 2 units or fewer are 28% of the units and 44% of the margin.

Bulk was under cost

The usual 12.5-unit sale was RM 750. The Floor with unpaid salt in it is RM 760.

Price barely moved demand

Across 18 full weeks, a week priced 10% higher sold about 3% fewer units (estimate, weak: correlation -0.09). After paying over 8% above the usual price, 76% of buyers came back within 30 days, against 78% at the usual price and 94% at a low one.

But high prices cost orders

Orders priced over 8% above usual were cancelled 12% of the time against 6.5% at the usual price, and the next order after a high price was smaller: 1.8 units against 2.7.

You already price scarcity

With 3 days of stock or fewer (42 of 137 days) sales went at 7% over the usual price; with over 14 days of stock, 4% under. Sales a day did not fall when stock was short (4.7 units against 5.1). Estimate: the daily shelf is rebuilt from purchases, handovers and counts.

Floor

True cost, the price you never sell under

Live since v1496. No change proposed.
  1. Rule. Cost a unit (the latest purchase's quote plus its freight, over its units) times 1 plus the unpaid share, times the size, the order total up to the next RM 5.
  2. The unpaid share is count shortfall, gifts and rewards, and written-off salt, over the units paid for. Today 114.34 + 15.39 + 2.50 over 620.92 units: 21.3%.
  3. Who pays it. A partner, at every size. On the new scale that is simply -100%.
  4. One refinement to settle. The share ticks with every handover. Re-measuring it only at a stock count or a new lot would keep the Floor still between them (Ask H).

Median Price

The price that earns the most, moved by the market

Today's Set Price, renamed in every word you read. Customers never see the word. Identifiers in code stay.

Step 1. Where it starts

It starts at today's prices, the 20% rise of 10 Nov: RM 75, 120, 170, 225, 275, 320, 510, 625 and 995. The evidence base of draft 1 (RM 70, 105, 155, 200, 245, 285, 450, 505, 845) is lower, read from sales made before the rise. Running today's prices for four weeks and then reading the result is worth more than choosing between the two now (Ask A).

Step 2. Kept inside the band, and asked when it is not

  1. The rule. The Median is never under the Floor and never over the Ceiling. The market move and a customer's % are held there without asking.
  2. A figure you type is different. If you type a Median point, or a price on one order, that falls outside the band, the app stops and says by how much and what it means in ringgit: "RM 30 under the Floor: a loss of RM 30 on this order" or "RM 20 over the Ceiling". It then asks once whether you mean it.
  3. On your yes it stands, marked. The order is filed as a one-off with your reason and left out of the evidence, so one favour never drags the Median. A Median point outside the band stands until you change it and shows on Pricing every day as outside.
  4. The month's tally. Pricing shows how many one-offs were made in the last 30 days and what they gave away or gained.

Step 3. The market move, supply and demand

Each day the Median moves along the scale by a few points. Supply is the days of stock cover the Desk already measures for the restock light. Demand is the pace of the last 14 days against the last 56.

  1. Limits. The two add, held between -15 and +20 points, moving at most 5 points a day, so a price never jumps overnight.
  2. What it does to a price. At 1 unit RM 120 becomes RM 125 when stock is short and RM 115 when it is long; at 2.5 units RM 275 becomes RM 290 or RM 260.
  3. An agreed order never moves, and a partner never moves: the Floor is the Floor in any market.

Step 4. Kept at the profit-best price

  1. A standing reading on Pricing. For each size, over the last 90 days: the usual price paid, the profit-best price, and the Median beside them, with the count of sales behind each.
  2. A re-anchor is your tap. When the reading and the Median differ by RM 5 or more at a size with 10 or more sales, the Desk suggests the new point. Nothing re-anchors by itself.
  3. Losses are counted. Cancellations and smaller next orders after a high price are read into the same reading.
  4. Every quote is recorded from here on. Today the book keeps only what sold and the lost-demand log is empty. A quote that did not become an order is the one record that turns a cautious estimate into a measured demand curve.

New buyers are drying up

20 in July, 11 in August, 6 in September, 3 in October. The last 28 days sold 108.8 units against 152 in the 28 before, and earned RM 4,273 against RM 5,315. Margin a unit has risen every month; volume has not.

Regulars are the business

20 of the 46 buyers have come four times or more, and they are 82% of the margin. The ten largest are 62%. 16 buyers came once.

Their habits are predictable

A regular's usual gap is 8 days, and 64% of their orders came within half to one and a half times their own usual gap. The day a customer should next order can be named in advance.

14 regulars are late today

16 are on time. 8 are late by their own habit and 6 have lapsed (three times their gap, or 21 days). The late and lapsed earned about RM 3,300 a 30 days at their past rate (estimate). Four of the ten largest are late and one has lapsed.

Late is the moment to act

Of 52 times a regular ran past one and a half times their gap, they came back 40 times (77%). Once lapsed, 6 of 11 (55%).

Do not push small buyers to bulk

Five buyers take 1 unit or less every week or sooner and earn you RM 47 a unit. Five regular bulk buyers earn RM 22 a unit. Selling the first group bigger lots would halve what each unit earns.

No season can be read yet

Fridays earn the most (RM 208 a day) and Sundays the least (RM 101). The part of the month makes little difference (RM 147 to RM 177 a day). Around four public holidays in the window there is no common pattern. 137 days hold no full year, so a season has to be marked first and measured as it passes.

Dependable

What keeps it safe on your only income

  1. Hard limits. No automatic move leaves the band, exceeds -15 to +20 points, or moves more than 5 points in a day. An agreed order is never repriced.
  2. A reason for every move, shown on Pricing and kept in a log: "Median +5: 4.2 days of cover".
  3. One switch. Off returns every price to the plain Median at once.
  4. Proved on the past before it runs. The build replays all 137 days through the rules and shows what it would have quoted each day, beside what you did quote.
  5. A weekly scorecard. Margin a day against the last eight weeks, orders, new buyers, late regulars won back, and what the market move added or cost (estimate). If margin a day falls two weeks running while the move is up, the move stands down and says so.
  6. It suggests where it has not measured. A signal acts by itself only once it has a measured basis; until then it is your tap.
  7. What it cannot promise. The best price is an estimate from 46 buyers. The engine makes fewer unforced errors and catches a late customer or a thin week early; it cannot make the market larger.
Limits

What the data cannot say

  1. 46 buyers, and five of them are 40% of the margin. A handful of people set these curves.
  2. Only prices that sold are in the book. The profit-best figures are floors on the true answer.
  3. The 20% rise is one day old. Nothing here measures it yet.
  4. Bulk has 10 sales, usually at RM 750. At a Median of RM 995 a bulk buyer will mostly need a negative %, which can reach RM 760 and no lower.
  5. Demand's response to price is weak and noisy inside about 15% either side of usual, and unknown outside it.
  6. No full year. Seasons are marked by you and learnt as they pass.
  7. The holiday dates used (25 Aug, 31 Aug, 16 Sep, 08 Nov) are an assumption from the public calendar.

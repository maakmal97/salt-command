The short version

Every approval tapped becomes a whole release: the row is written into a file in GitHub, the app is rebuilt and redeployed, and the full suite runs. That happened 53 times in a week. It works, but it is slow and keeps the real ledger in a public repo.

The plan moves the book into one database inside Cloudflare, so a tap books the row in about a second and a version goes back to meaning a change to the code. It is done in place, one small step at a time, and every step before the switch can be undone with a revert.

Aims

  • Tap Approve and every figure on the Desk and the Counter counts the row within about a second (S4).
  • Nothing is rebuilt because something was approved; a version means a code change (S5).
  • Tests check the code on a fixed copy of the book; live-data rules still run on the real one (S0, done).
  • A nightly backup, and a daily export to git as the audit trail (S0b, S5).
  • The fold's notes still written, just after the row (S5).

Approvals, pre-approvals, refusals and the vault pair, encrypted to R2. The bucket and the job each need his yes. 1 session.

S2 · Later

The Book as a shadow copy

Refilled at every fold and checked against git on rows, stock, counts, notes and pricing. Nothing reads it yet. 4 to 6 sessions; way back: remove it.

S3 · Later

Read from the Book

First "on its way" becomes approved but not yet in the Book, so nothing counts twice; then the drafter and order code read it. 3 to 5 sessions; way back: switch to D1.

S4 · Later

A tap books the row at once

Every check runs, then the row books in the Book; the Book issues the row id and writes it back so the fold copies it. 6 to 9 sessions; way back: switch off.

S5 · Later

The Book becomes the record

After 7 days of agreement. The fold becomes a daily export to git; approvals stop being versions. 6 to 9 sessions; way back 60 to 90 min (estimate).

S6 · Later

Orders and the book in step

The Book hands order moves to the Counter's order book through an outbox. 3 to 5 sessions.

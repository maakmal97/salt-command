One price rule for every book: the price that makes the most profit

Every book prices from the same five parameters: supply cost, demand cost, expected margin, your market-intel price and the tested market price. A price never goes under its Floor (all it costs to sell the unit) or over its Ceiling (what your intel says the market bears). It starts at your intel price. For eight weeks at a time, the whole list runs 10% up one week and 10% down another. When both halves of the test show the same side making more profit, the engine proposes moving the whole list 10% that way, and your Approve books it. The price climbs towards the most profit and stops where a step either way earns the same.

Why it makes the most profit three lines to justify it

  • Profit is margin times volume. Profit a day is the price less the all-in cost, times the units sold at that price. A higher price earns more on each unit and sells fewer. The best price is where one step up loses as many ringgit in lost orders as it gains on the orders kept.
  • The test measures exactly that trade. It runs on the book's own buyers, at a step either side of today's list, and it is read on what the whole book earns. Moving towards the side that earned more is climbing the hill. When both sides earn the same, the list is at the top, and it stays there.
  • Three guards keep it safe. The Floor means no sale loses money. The Ceiling keeps the price inside what your intel says the market bears. Both halves must agree, so one lucky or slow week cannot move a price.
Where salt's top isSalt's whole list earns MYR 143 a day after all-in cost, on 13.4 orders a week (partners left out, last 84 days). It is at its top if each 1% on the price loses 2.78% of orders. If it loses fewer, raising pays; if more, lowering pays. One size alone turns at a different point: 1 unit at 1.91%, 12.5 units at 3.71%. That is why the whole list is tested, not one size. History cannot say which side salt is on. Its 1-unit price rose from MYR 90 to MYR 130 across five boards, while orders went from 4.5 a week to 6.2, then 6.0, 4.7 and 4.7, and the customer base changed at the same time. So the test leads.   • Near the top no test can tell, and near the top it matters least. When the list is close to its best, the two sides earn nearly the same. The halves then often disagree and the list holds, and a move either way earns about the same. Minus 3.1% at a 10% step is the most the rule gives up when today's list is already right.
  • The order of the weeks cancels a trend. With demand growing 10% a week, plain alternation (up, down, up, down) leans the read towards down: 35% of tests move down and 16% move up, where the two sides truly earn the same. The order up, down, down, up, then down, up, up, down gives 25% each way. Estimate Fable's seeded runs, re-run and matched.
  • The guard counts both sides together. A guard of 15 orders on each side would void 27% to 56% of salt's tests at 1 unit, mostly the ones where the up side sold little, which is the evidence for down. So the walk would lean up. Thirty orders in all, both sides together, does not lean either way.

The engine's priceReading finds the price that earned most in the last 90 days, counting the orders refused: MYR 100 at 1 unit, MYR 185 at 2 units and MYR 420 at 5 units, against Medians of MYR 130, MYR 245 and MYR 540. It only sees the prices customers were asked, mostly the lower ones of the older boards, so it cannot see a higher price working. Verified From priceReading on today's book.

How the engine does it the parts it already has, joined

  • The rules (engine/rules.mjs). One PROFIT_RULE object holds every book's parameters:
    • demand: shrink with gifts off: salt's measured share, a fixed 20% on oil and rice (ask 9), and none on candy and gas until you say; delivery and handling in MYR; credit on;
    • test: a 10% step (ask 4), 8 weeks, the order UDDUDUUD, 30 orders in all, both halves must agree, salt only.
    Your intel stays where it is: salt's four points, the supplier's Ceiling and each typed board. Two lists must name the new object, or the gate and the suite refuse it: tools/ledger.mjs NOT_LEDGER, and the NAMES list in test/engine-rules.mjs. The small-lot plan has the same trap.
  • The Floor (engine/snapshot.mjs nrsShare, and engine/pricing.mjs costStack and floorTotal). RULES.nrsFloor is read from PROFIT_RULE.demand, with gifts off. Delivery and handling come back through the cost stack's per-order line, which stands at MYR 0 today as timePerOrder. Credit enters as a share of the price.
  • The test (testOf and testReading). The test moves the whole list by the step, in the order, on top of the Median, before the Floor and the Ceiling clamp each size. The reading gives both halves and the whole book's all-in profit a day, with partners out and days with no free stock left out. The engine's shelfAt gives those days from the book.
  • The move is booked, never replayed. At a test's end, Salt Admin's price place shows the verdict and the proposed move. Your Approve books it once as a MARKET entry, the same road as your switch and your points. The list's level is one plus the step, raised to the moves up less the moves down, so an up and a down return every price. A later correction to an old row, or a new lot, never changes a verdict already booked, and the calendar (kept in the Counter's store, not the book) is not read. The price is then a function of the book alone: the same book gives the same price on every device.
  • The market move by stock cover is held at nought, during a test and between tests, because its swing at 1 unit (MYR 115 to MYR 135) is as large as the step and would read as demand. Its reading stays on the card (ask 8).
  • Their % rides on top as today, through bandTop. A plus place narrows as the level rises, because the Ceiling does not move: at 1 unit, +30% is MYR 10 over the Median at MYR 130, and nothing over it at MYR 175, the Ceiling.
  • Who shows it.
    • Salt Admin's price place gets one card per book: this week's side, profit a day on each side in each half, the verdict, the move to approve, and the day of the next one.
    • The drafter's note names the level and the week's side.
    • The Desk's pricing pane shows the same card.
    • Salt Counter shows prices only, with no new words.
  • Unchanged: a partner pays the Floor; a figure under the Floor is asked about once; dated records keep their prices; and the small-lot plan's supply cost slots in, whichever lands first.

How it is proved

  • A new section, "the price that makes the most profit" (test/profit-engine.mjs). Each check is proved red by its own mutation before its green counts.
  • Nothing else moves. On the suite's frozen book (test/fixtures/book-frozen.json, ending 15 Oct 2026), every price at nought or a plus place is identical to today's. With no move booked and no test running, every price on today's book is identical too, apart from those that rest on the Floor.
  • The verdict, on literal weeks.
    • The up side wins both halves: a move up by one step is proposed.
    • The halves split: it holds.
    • Under 30 orders in all: it holds.
    • A day with no free stock: left out.
    • At the Ceiling or the Floor: it holds there.
    • A row corrected after a move is booked: the level does not change.
    • An up and a down booked: every price returns to the one before.
    • No test runs between a verdict and your answer.
  • Mutations that must go red:
    • deciding on all 8 weeks instead of the halves;
    • plain alternation instead of the order;
    • a guard on each side instead of the total;
    • profit on the booked cost instead of the all-in cost;
    • partners counted in the read;
    • one size moved instead of the whole list;
    • a level that takes a price under the Floor;
    • gifts left in the shrink;
    • a level replayed from the rows instead of read from the moves booked;
    • a move down of 0.9 instead of one over 1.1;
    • a test started before your answer.
  • Determinism: the same book read twice, and the page against the module through the drafter, give the same level and the same prices. Pricing parity stays equal.
  • Then the full suite, CI's run on the push, and the Counter drawn with counter-render to read the prices.

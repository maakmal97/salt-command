Two facts from the code shape it. A plain change of an agreed order wakes nobody today, so a raise after Accept is silent, while the same raise at Accept needs their yes. And the order book already takes more than Salt Admin offers: it can lower what is paid and handed over, and amend a completed order. Most of the gap is in the sheet, not underneath.

The one sheet one Change row, every stage

One row, Change, on every order card at every stage, opening one sheet in three groups, each field showing what it was and what it will be.

  • OrderWay, units, goods, delivery, when, and the point or spot
  • So farHanded over, paid
  • EndCancel or decline; reopen on a completed order

A line at the foot says what Save will do before you tap it: they will be told, told as a discount, not told, or needs their yes. One Save sends the change, then the handover, then the money, in that order.

Telling the customer one rule

Every change to what the customer sees is told; the word is discount when it is cheaper for the same or more goods. One check in the order book decides it for each change and stamps it on the move, so the wake follows from the move itself.

Which change is told, and how
The change Examples Banner
Changes their order Goods or delivery up, deliver becomes collect, an earlier window, fewer units New kind changed: "Your order has changed. Open it to see what is different."
A discount Owed less for the same or more units, the delivery charge taken off New kind discount: "You have received a discount on your order. Open your order to see it."
Bookkeeping A payment's method, the cost, the road, the book's own restate Not told
Already told An undo, paid, handed over, point moved, an offer, a cancellation The banner each has today, unchanged

The banner rule stands: no amount, product or name in a banner. The order page shows what was and what is.

The road, and its traps

A row and its order are tied by the order's ledgerKey, the engine's ovKey (party|date|goods plus delivery, then |product on any book but salt). Nothing goes from a row to an order today, and nothing is placed again: Order for them runs an Accept, and an Accept drafts a new Pending row.

So each order is born told: its queued already says what the row holds, so orderWork (stmt/orders.js) owes the book nothing. Fable found four places where that alone is not enough, each now a part below:

  • Old undo steps. undoFixes (src/orders.js) sends every past undo on a row an order names. s297 carries one: its order would drop to nothing paid, reopen and wake the customer. The import marks every past undo of its row as sent.
  • A close or redate. queued.close with no Close draft holds the order in flight for good (closeOnItsWay). An imported order carries the row's present size and goods, and never closed, queued.close or queued.redate.
  • A key that moves. A Correction of total, delivery or date moves the row's key. An order follows a moved key only through its acknowledgement draft, and an imported order has none. It stores its row's rid, and the pass and the tie-out find its row by rid first.
  • The acknowledgement's day. With no draft, pendingDay reads the day from the id. Stamped today, every imported order would look like a Pending row of today to Accept's twin check. It is stamped on the row's own day, in a form no draft id can equal.

What it changes, and what it never does

What the customer gains. Those rows leave Earlier and stand as orders, with Order again on a done one. A book Correction later reaches the order and tells them, as on any site order.

At import, never:

  • a banner to the customer or a ring on Salt Admin;
  • a "How was your order" (born with rateAsk set);
  • a place in the confirm time (its history is born with no placed and acknowledged pair, so no Accept is measured);
  • a moved price, Floor, reward, loyalty count or row; the book's road stays as written, a dated record.

Read off orders, so it does move (corrected: the first draft said otherwise). The chase reads orders (isAdvance, graceOver on movedOn), and so do cash on handover and the price ask. An imported order at Ready with goods out and money owed is chased, at once three times a day given its old movedOn, withholds cash on handover and refuses a price ask. This touches the 3 owing rows alone (Ask E).

The five open orders. Kept as it is: with 3 rows at most, an exemption is not worth its code in four readers.

The proof, and the way back

Each new check is proved red by its own mutation. The pass is driven with its told mark cleared, since a pass at an unchanged version returns early and would prove nothing. Right after an import it must tell nothing, follow nothing, send no undo, and leave a tie-out clean with nothing waiting. Then:

  • a Correction of an imported row's total and of its date restates its order;
  • s297's past undo is never sent;
  • a second import of one row lands once;
  • an imported order neither rings nor asks for a rating;
  • Accept's twin check reads no imported order as a Pending row of today.

After the live run: Salt Admin's Ledger check stays clean, node tools/standing.mjs reads the same seven lines, and the Counter's Earlier list holds only the rows that stayed. The way back is unimport over the same rids; the book never moved.

Said, not built: once imported, a live row beside a cancelled twin on one key reads as two rows, since the tie-break prefers a row whose road is the site. A new clash is refused at a change of goods or delivery, a close or a redate (keyClash), so this stays latent.
